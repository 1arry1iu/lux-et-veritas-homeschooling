# Mastery Mathematics
## 掌握式数学

**A mastery-based homeschool and self-study architecture from number sense to advanced mathematics.**  
**从数感与算术到高等数学的掌握式家庭教育与自主学习体系。**

---

## Purpose <br /> 目标

Mastery Mathematics is the mathematics curriculum architecture of **Lux et Veritas Homeschooling**. It is designed for families, independent learners, tutors, and small learning communities that want mathematical depth, durable understanding, and growing independence.

The sequence is deliberately more ambitious than a minimum school syllabus, but it is **not a race and not a requirement that every learner reach every stage**. Learners may enter after placement, slow down, compress already-mastered material, or stop at a level appropriate to their goals.

Broad progression:

> **Number & arithmetic → algebra + Euclidean geometry → advanced algebra + trigonometry → proof + discrete mathematics → calculus + linear algebra → multivariable mathematics + differential equations + probability → rigorous analysis → optional specialization**

For many learners, **Stages 1–4 form a strong secondary mathematical foundation**. Stages 5–6 are suitable for advanced STEM preparation. Stages 7–8 are specialist mathematics, not universal homeschool requirements.

---

## Design Principles <br /> 设计原则

1. **Mastery before acceleration.** Move forward when important prerequisites are secure enough to support the next ideas.
2. **Understanding and fluency both matter.** Learners should know what to do, why it works, and when it is useful.
3. **Problems dominate study time.** Reading prepares the learner to do mathematics; solving, proving, checking, revising, and explaining produce mathematical competence.
4. **Algebra and geometry develop together.** Geometry strengthens proof, visualization, construction, and mathematical judgment.
5. **Proof begins gradually.** Explanation, counterexample, induction, contradiction, invariants, and Euclidean argument should develop before university.
6. **Depth beats book completion.** A book is a tool, not a checklist.
7. **Retrieval and mixed review are permanent.** Previously learned ideas return after delays and in varied contexts.
8. **Difficulty is layered.** Include routine fluency, stretching problems, and a smaller number of genuinely difficult tasks.
9. **Communication counts.** Good mathematics includes notation, diagrams, definitions, assumptions, reasoning, and precise conclusions.
10. **Technology follows foundations.** Estimation, mental arithmetic, symbolic fluency, graph sense, and hand reasoning should exist before routine dependence on calculators, CAS, coding, or AI.
11. **Curiosity is part of the curriculum.** Puzzles, history, biography, mathematical games, modeling, and open problems sustain intellectual life.
12. **Independence is taught.** The learner should gradually need less prompting, not merely complete harder content.

## Instructional Progression <br /> 教学进阶

For genuinely new material, do not force discovery when the learner lacks the knowledge needed to discover efficiently.

A useful progression is:

**teacher/mentor model → worked example → guided practice → partially completed example → independent practice → delayed retrieval → mixed problem → transfer task**

As competence increases, fade the support. For familiar domains, shift more time toward non-routine problems, proofs, projects, oral explanation, mathematical writing, coding, and modeling.

## Eight-Stage Pathway <br /> 八阶段路径

| Stage | Main mathematical work | Representative spines | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Number, Arithmetic & Mathematical Habits** | Whole numbers, fractions, decimals, ratio, percent, estimation, divisibility, mental arithmetic, word problems, early variables | Kiselev — *Arithmetic*; Berezanskaya — *Arithmetic—Problems and Exercises* | Accurate mixed arithmetic; good estimation; multi-step word problems; can persist and explain strategies |
| **2 — Algebra I + Euclidean Geometry I** | Expressions, equations, inequalities, systems, polynomials, factorization, functions, congruence, similarity, circles, area, introductory proof | Gelfand & Shen — *Algebra*; Kiselev — *Geometry: Planimetry* | Algebraic fluency plus coherent multi-step geometric arguments; selects relevant results with decreasing prompting |
| **3 — Algebra II, Trigonometry, Coordinate & Solid Geometry** | Quadratics, rational/radical equations, exponentials/logarithms, trigonometry, vectors, coordinate geometry, stereometry | Kiselev — *Algebra II*; Gelfand & Saul — *Trigonometry*; Kiselev — *Stereometry*; selected analytic geometry | Moves among symbolic, graphical, geometric, and verbal representations and chooses strategies in mixed work |
| **4 — Proof, Discrete Mathematics, Number Theory & Precalculus Maturity** | Logic, sets, quantifiers, proof methods, counting, induction, invariants, congruences, Diophantine equations, sequences, inequalities | Hammack — *Book of Proof*; Borovik & Gardiner; Vilenkin; selected *Mathematics for Computer Science* | Proof portfolio spanning several methods and domains; can explain and defend reasoning orally |
| **5 — Single-Variable Calculus + Linear Algebra** | Limits, continuity, derivatives, integrals, Taylor approximation, vectors, vector spaces, linear transformations, eigenvalues | Apostol — *Calculus, Vol. 1* or Spivak — *Calculus*; Shilov — *Linear Algebra* | Solves mixed calculus problems independently, explains hypotheses, creates examples/counterexamples, and uses linear algebra conceptually |
| **6 — Multivariable Calculus, Vector Analysis, Differential Equations & Probability** | Partial derivatives, multiple integrals, vector fields, integral theorems, ODEs, random variables, expectation, distributions, LLN/CLT ideas | Apostol — *Calculus, Vol. 2*; selected vector-analysis text; Pontryagin — *Ordinary Differential Equations*; Gnedenko/Khinchin or Rozanov | Integrated modeling project connecting several mathematical tools with assumptions, derivation, computation, and interpretation |
| **7 — Rigorous Analysis & Advanced Core** | Epsilon-delta reasoning, sequences and series, compactness, completeness, theorem-centered learning, deeper linear algebra and selected abstract structures | Kolmogorov & Fomin — *Introductory Real Analysis*; Rudin — *Principles of Mathematical Analysis*; selected advanced companions | Independently reads theorem-centered material, reconstructs proofs, creates examples/counterexamples, and writes a substantial exposition |
| **8 — Specialization** | One primary advanced track, optionally a secondary track | Track dependent | Substantial project, advanced problem portfolio, computational notebook, expository paper, or research-style study |

> [!TIP]
> **Acceleration should compress repetition, not delete foundations.** A learner who reaches calculus early but cannot reason geometrically, prove elementary claims, estimate, or repair errors independently is not meaningfully accelerated.

## Stage 8 Specialization <br /> 第八阶段专业方向

Possible tracks:

| Track | Focus | Representative core |
|---|---|---|
| **Discrete Mathematics, CS & Algorithms** | Combinatorics, recurrences, graphs, algorithms, computational experiments | Graham, Knuth & Patashnik; *Mathematics for Computer Science* |
| **Statistics & Machine Learning** | Statistical learning, probabilistic modeling, mathematical foundations of ML | Deisenroth et al.; James et al.; Bishop; Murphy |
| **Optimization & Control** | Convex and numerical optimization, variational methods, optimal control | Kochenderfer & Wheeler; Ben-Tal & Nemirovski; Gelfand & Fomin |
| **Probability, Stochastic Processes & Information** | Probability theory, random walks, Markov processes, entropy, coding | Feller; Gnedenko; Dynkin; MacKay; Yeung |
| **Mathematical Physics** | Mathematical methods, mechanics/physics problem solving, PDE ideas, generalized functions | Feynman; Irodov; Vladimirov; Gelfand & Shilov |
| **Pure Mathematics** | Number theory, algebra, topology, real/complex analysis, differential geometry | Hardy & Wright; Lang; Kelley; Shilov; selected advanced texts |

Specialization should follow the learner's goals and sustained interest. It is not a prestige ladder.

## Placement <br /> 定位

Do not place by age alone. Place at the highest level for which the learner can meet the preceding stage's essential outcomes **without heavy scaffolding**.

A diagnostic can sample:

- whole-number arithmetic, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios, estimation, mental arithmetic;
- equations, factorization, exponents, identities, inequalities, functions;
- Euclidean geometry, congruence/similarity, area, construction, elementary proof;
- trigonometry and coordinate geometry;
- sequences, combinatorics, elementary number theory, proof techniques;
- for advanced entry: limits, derivatives, integrals, matrices, vectors, and probability.

When knowledge is uneven, use a **targeted bridge** instead of repeating an entire year or book. Repair the smallest missing prerequisites, reassess, and return to the main sequence.

## Mastery Evidence <br /> 掌握证据

No single percentage defines mastery.

Use an evidence bundle:

- **Accuracy:** routine techniques are dependable.
- **Reasoning:** the learner can justify important steps and identify relevant definitions/results.
- **Retention:** important knowledge remains available after a meaningful delay.
- **Transfer:** methods adapt to unfamiliar problems rather than only familiar templates.
- **Communication:** notation, diagrams, prose, and proof structure are understandable.
- **Independence:** the learner can select methods, check work, recover from errors, and seek help appropriately.

Numerical thresholds may be used as **signals**, not as the definition of mastery. For example, families may expect roughly 90% accuracy on representative routine work and 80% on difficult mixed/transfer work, but those figures should never overrule evidence about problem difficulty, retention, explanation, and independence.

## Time Model & Weekly Rhythm <br /> 时间模型与每周节奏

Time should follow developmental readiness, attention, and mathematical level rather than a rigid universal schedule.

Typical focused study ranges:

| Level | Approximate focused mathematics time |
|---|---:|
| Foundation | 45–75 min/day, about 5 days/week |
| Secondary | 75–105 min/day, about 5 days/week |
| Calculus and beyond | 90–150 min/day, about 5 days/week |

These are planning ranges, not minimum quotas. Younger learners may do better with shorter sessions separated by movement and reading.

A balanced week usually includes:

- new concepts and guided practice;
- independent practice;
- mixed retrieval from older topics;
- geometry/proof/discrete reasoning;
- correction and re-solving of errors;
- one non-routine or challenge session;
- occasional mathematical culture, coding, modeling, history, or project work.

Most study time should be spent **doing mathematics**, not merely consuming explanations.

## Assessment System <br /> 评估体系

### Daily
- Check work without erasing the original attempt.
- Record important errors: **topic → error type → corrected reasoning → later retrieval**.
- Include a small amount of older material.

### Weekly
- Complete one mixed set without immediate notes/solutions.
- Produce one polished solution to a non-routine problem.
- Explain a definition, theorem, proof idea, or solution orally.

### Every 4–8 weeks
- Use a cumulative mastery check with routine, mixed, and transfer items.
- Repair and reassess missed prerequisites.
- Add representative work to a cumulative portfolio.

### Every term
- Complete a cumulative assessment or substantial problem set.
- Defend selected solutions orally.
- Complete a project, modeling task, or mathematical exposition.
- Reflect on strengths, recurring errors, effective strategies, and the next target.

## Self-Study Problem-Solving Protocol <br /> 自学习题协议

1. State what is given and what must be found or proved.
2. Try a diagram, small case, estimate, numerical experiment, or simpler analogue.
3. Recall relevant definitions, theorems, identities, and previously solved structures.
4. Work independently while **new mathematical progress is still occurring**.
5. If stuck in a loop, take the smallest useful hint rather than a complete solution.
6. After studying a solution, close it and reconstruct the argument from memory.
7. Re-solve important problems after a delay.
8. Record reusable ideas in a strategy notebook: substitutions, constructions, inequalities, invariants, transformations, proof patterns, counterexamples, and common error types.

Time-based struggle targets can be useful, but they are secondary to the quality of thinking. Persistence is valuable; unproductive repetition is not.

## Role of the Parent, Tutor, or Mentor <br /> 家长与导师角色

- Protect consistent study time without turning every hard problem into a lecture.
- Model new methods when the learner lacks prerequisite schemas.
- Ask questions that return thinking to the learner: *What do you know? What is the target? What representation might help? Which assumption has not been used?*
- Require correction and later re-solving of significant errors.
- Occasionally require oral explanation so copied work cannot masquerade as understanding.
- Keep challenge high enough to produce growth but not so high that confusion becomes the normal state.
- Permit genuine acceleration while preserving foundations, geometry, proof, communication, and mathematical culture.
- Diagnose the smallest missing prerequisite when progress breaks down.
- Fade prompts as competence grows.

## Capstone Milestones <br /> 阶段性成果

| After stage | Suggested capstone |
|---|---|
| **1** | Mental-arithmetic and estimation portfolio plus original multi-step word problems |
| **2** | Euclidean geometry portfolio containing polished proofs and constructions |
| **3** | Model a real situation using algebra, trigonometry, and coordinates; test the model numerically and graphically |
| **4** | Proof portfolio plus oral presentation/defense of selected problems |
| **5** | Short exposition linking an idea from calculus to an idea from linear algebra |
| **6** | Differential-equation or probability modeling project with assumptions, derivation, computation, and interpretation |
| **7** | Independent reading of theorem-centered mathematics followed by a substantial expository paper |
| **8** | Research-style exposition, computational notebook, advanced problem portfolio, or modeling study |

Capstones are evidence of synthesis and transfer, not extra decoration after "real" study.

## Using the Book Library <br /> 如何使用书库

Do not turn the bibliography into a reading checklist. Assign each resource a role:

| Role | Use |
|---|---|
| **Spine** | Main explanatory sequence; study substantially and systematically |
| **Problem bank** | Additional practice, mixed work, and challenge problems |
| **Proof / problem-solving laboratory** | Non-routine reasoning, ingenuity, and proof habits |
| **Enrichment / culture** | History, puzzles, biography, recreational mathematics, conceptual perspective |
| **Reference / advanced extension** | Selective consultation after prerequisites are secure |

**Operating rule: use one main spine per major strand at a time. More books do not automatically produce more learning.**

## Final Operating Rule <br /> 最终运行原则

> **Keep the spine small, the supply of good problems rich, the standards for reasoning high, and the learner's curiosity alive. Prefer durable understanding over speed, revisit meaningful errors, and gradually transfer responsibility for learning to the learner.**
